first_action: If water starts to rise or you are told to evacuate, leave right away.
avoid:
- Do not drive into flooded roads or around a barricade. Turn around, don't drown.
- Just 6 inches of moving water is enough to knock you off your feet.
- 12 inches of water can float a car or small SUV. 18 inches of water can carry away large vehicles.
- Do not go into a basement or any room if water covers the electrical outlets or if cords are under water.
- Never use a portable generator inside your home or garage.
- Do not enter a flood-damaged home or building until the authorities give the All Clear.
- Gas leaks and live power lines can be deadly, but they are not obvious at first glance.
steps:
- If water starts to rise or you are told to evacuate, leave right away.
- If you live in a flood-prone area or are camping in a low-lying area, get to higher ground right away.
- Stay informed. Listen to radio and TV, including NOAA Weather Radio if possible. Check the internet and social media for updates.
- If you see sparks or hear buzzing, crackling, snapping or popping noises, get out.
- If you are trapped by moving water, move to the highest possible point. Call your local emergency number if possible.
- Floodwaters often become contaminated with sewage or chemicals. Make sure water is safe to drink, cook or clean with after a flood.
- Let your family and close friends know you are okay so they can help spread the word.
