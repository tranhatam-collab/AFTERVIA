first_action: If a person has signs of heat stroke, call your local emergency number or get them to a hospital immediately.
avoid:
- Never leave a child, a person with a disability, or a pet locked in a car, even in winter.
- Do not give fluids to a person who has heat stroke.
- A fan blowing air toward someone may make them hotter when heat index temperatures are above about 37°C.
steps:
- 'Check in with friends and relatives in these groups: young children and infants, older adults, people with long-term health conditions, and pregnant women. Check in especially with those who do not have air conditioning.'
- If a toddler lives with you, lock your cars, even in your own driveway. Children may play in cars or wander into them, and can die in 10 minutes.
- It is important to know the symptoms of extreme heat exposure and the right response.
- Heat cramps are painful muscle cramps and spasms, usually in the legs and belly, with heavy sweating. They may be the first sign of heat-related illness, and may lead to heat exhaustion or stroke. Press firmly on the cramping muscle or gently massage it to ease the spasm. Give sips of water unless the person complains of nausea, then stop giving water. Get medical help right away if cramps last longer than 1 hour.
- Signs of heat exhaustion include heavy sweating, weakness or tiredness, cool, pale, clammy skin, a fast weak pulse, muscle cramps, dizziness, nausea or vomiting, headache, and fainting. For heat exhaustion, move the person to a cooler place and loosen their clothing. Apply cool, wet cloths, or have them sit in a cool bath. Offer sips of water. Get medical help right away if the person vomits, their symptoms get worse, or their symptoms last longer than 1 hour.
- Heat stroke is a severe medical emergency. Call your local emergency number or get the person to a hospital immediately, because delay can be deadly. Signs of heat stroke include a throbbing headache, confusion, slurred speech, nausea, dizziness, a body temperature above 39.4°C, hot red dry or damp skin, a rapid strong pulse, fainting, and becoming unconscious.
- For heat stroke, move the person to a cooler place, ideally somewhere with air conditioning. Reduce their body temperature with cool cloths or a bath. Use a fan if heat index temperatures are below about 37°C. Above about 37°C, a fan may actually make a person hotter.
